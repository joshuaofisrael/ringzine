WWE president Nick Khan has picked out the wrestler he thinks will lead the company's next era. "He's sort of our next big thing, if you will, and again, there's never been a wrestler who's looked or felt like him," Khan said of Oba Femi, in comments [Fightful posted on X](https://x.com/Fightful/status/2123444764204744991) on Thursday.

## Where Khan said it

Khan was speaking on Bloomberg's The Deal podcast with Jason Kelly and Alex Rodriguez at the Screentime conference, [Ringside News reports](https://www.ringsidenews.com/nick-khan-names-oba-femi-wwes-next-big-thing/). The conversation also covered WWE's media rights and TKO's wider plans, according to [PWInsider](https://www.pwinsider.com/article/213840/wwe-president-nick-khan-discusses-how-the-netflix-deal-was-made-why-he-never-wants-to-lose-the-core-wwe-fan-base-recruiting-talents-from-universities-and-more.html?p=1), which published a fuller transcript.

## Why there is no next John Cena

Khan's point was that WWE stopped trying to copy its old stars. "So the next one never looks like the last one," he said, according to Ringside News. "There's no next John Cena. He's one of one."

He said WWE shifted its recruiting about six years ago, coming out of COVID, from mainly mining the independent scene to college athletics as well. He pointed to Roman Reigns at Georgia Tech, Big E at the University of Iowa and The Rock at the University of Miami as football players who came WWE's way.

## The Undertaker's campus pitch

Then WWE widened the net beyond football. Khan said the company sent The Undertaker onto campuses to tell male and female athletes, track and field included, that if the Olympics or the modern Wheaties box did not happen, "come this way".

That is how Femi arrived, in Khan's telling. "So one of our big superstars now is a wrestler named Oba Femi. He was a shot putter at the University of Alabama five years ago, and when Undertaker went to go speak to them, saw a big athlete, strong athlete," Khan said in the clip Fightful shared. Khan added that Femi grew up in Nigeria, did not play American football as a kid, but had a raw athletic skill set that could easily translate to WWE.
