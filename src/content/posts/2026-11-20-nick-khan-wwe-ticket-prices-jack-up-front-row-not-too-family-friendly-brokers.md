If you have winced at the price of a WWE ticket lately, the company's president has now explained the thinking out loud. The [Wrestling Observer flagged the comments on X](https://x.com/WONF4W/status/2123469245468054005) on Thursday under the line that Nick Khan "explains why WWE can't make ticket prices 'too family friendly.'"

## What Khan said

According to the [Wrestling Observer's write up](https://www.f4wonline.com/news/wwe/nick-khan-explains-why-wwe-cant-make-ticket-prices-too-family-friendly/), Khan was speaking at Bloomberg's Screentime conference with Alex Rodriguez and Jason Kelly of The Deal, and was asked how WWE balances what fans need with what the business needs.

He split the arena in two. For the priciest seats, which he called the P1 tickets, Khan said WWE wants to "jack those up as high as possible," adding that it is "no problem" for wealthy buyers like Rodriguez who can afford them. The clip [shared by Covalent TV](https://x.com/TheCovalentTV/status/2123474725607190774) carries the same quote.

The upper bowl is different, he said. "You want to say, 'Hey, we want to price it family friendly.' But if you price it too family friendly, they all go to third party brokers. That's how we do it."

## The reseller argument

Put simply, Khan's case is that seats priced well below what the market will pay get bought up by resale sellers and listed again at a markup, so fans end up paying more anyway, just not to WWE. The Observer summed it up as keeping prices above a level where everything ends up with resellers like StubHub.

Plenty of fans on X were not convinced, and the quote spread quickly on Thursday and Friday. What Khan did not give was any number for where that family friendly line sits.

## Where WWE goes, and why

Khan also explained how WWE picks host cities for its biggest shows, the Observer reports. Money is a big factor but not the only one: he pointed to taking SummerSlam to Minnesota this summer even though other cities might have earned more, because it mattered to reach Midwest fans. He added that WWE tries not to put too many shows in one region, which he said "keeps, obviously, the demand up."
