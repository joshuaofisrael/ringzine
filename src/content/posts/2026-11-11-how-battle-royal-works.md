A WWE over-the-top-rope battle royal is decided by eliminating opponents from the ring, not by scoring pinfalls. In the format WWE described for the Andre the Giant Memorial Battle Royal, all competitors begin inside the ring together. A wrestler is eliminated only after going over the top rope and having both feet touch the floor. The last competitor remaining wins.

## Everybody starts together

WWE’s official description of the 2016 Andre the Giant Memorial Battle Royal makes the starting procedure explicit: all participants are in the ring at the same time. That is an important distinction from WWE’s Royal Rumble format, in which entrants arrive in stages. The battle royal begins with a crowded field, and the number of active wrestlers generally decreases as eliminations happen.

A battle royal can have different field sizes. WWE announced a 50-person battle royal for Super ShowDown in 2019, describing the same over-the-top-rope elimination mechanism. WWE also advertised a SummerSlam battle royal in 2023 with both-feet-to-the-floor eliminations. Those official descriptions show that the core method can remain consistent while the size and stakes of the match change.

A crowded opening does not mean a competitor must eliminate someone immediately. Wrestlers may try to avoid the ropes, combine efforts to lift an opponent or wait for an opening. These are tactical consequences of the stated rules rather than separate victory conditions. No matter how many people help push somebody out, the elimination still depends on the announced rope-and-floor test.

## The two-part elimination test

The most important detail is that going outside the ring is not automatically the same as being eliminated. WWE’s published rules require both an exit over the top rope and both feet contacting the ringside floor. A wrestler who slips under the bottom rope has not satisfied the over-the-top-rope condition. A wrestler who goes over the top but avoids having both feet land has not yet met the floor condition either.

For example, imagine competitor A is pushed over the top rope but lands with one foot on the floor while keeping the other elevated. Under the both-feet standard, A is not eliminated at that moment. If A later puts the second foot down, the floor condition is satisfied. Conversely, if competitor B rolls beneath the bottom rope and stands outside, the required top-rope exit has not occurred. These examples explain the rule; actual calls depend on what officials observe in the match.

The apron is also worth watching. A competitor may be outside the ropes while still standing on the apron. WWE’s published test refers to both feet touching the ringside floor, not simply to standing beyond the ropes. The difference is why a wrestler can remain in contention while hanging onto the ropes or balancing on the edge of the ring.

## What does not decide the contest

A battle royal is not a pinfall tournament. A three-count against one wrestler is not the elimination method WWE identifies for this format. Likewise, making an opponent submit does not satisfy the published over-the-top-rope requirement. The relevant event is a qualifying elimination, and the referee’s attention is directed toward how the wrestler leaves the ring and where their feet land.

The contest also is not won by being the person who eliminates the most opponents. WWE’s rules say the last person standing wins. Suppose A eliminates five wrestlers but is then thrown out by B, and B is the final participant. B wins even though A accounted for more eliminations. The win condition rewards survival, not a running elimination total.

There is no universal entrant count embedded in the name “battle royal.” The 2016 Andre memorial edition and WWE’s 2019 50-person contest demonstrate that promotions can specify different lineups. The event announcement, rather than an assumption about a standard field size, determines how many wrestlers are involved.

## Battle royal versus other multi-person matches

The elimination rule is what separates this contest from other formats that also put many wrestlers together. In a WWE Elimination Chamber, for example, pinfall and submission eliminate participants; in a WWE over-the-top-rope battle royal, the decisive test is the top rope and both feet on the floor. In a conventional multi-person pinfall match, one successful fall may end the entire contest. Those are different rule structures, even when the ring appears similarly crowded.

For viewers, the clearest way to follow a battle royal is to track who remains eligible rather than counting near-falls or holds. Watch the top rope, the apron and the ringside floor. A dramatic throw is only a confirmed elimination when the announced conditions are met. Once every other participant has been eliminated, the final wrestler in contention is the winner.
